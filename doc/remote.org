#+TITLE: drover across hosts: design
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil

A design, accepted, its first four milestones built (see [[*The first milestone, as built][below]], [[*The second milestone, as built][after]], [[*The third milestone, as built][after that]] and [[*The fourth milestone, as built][the fourth]]), the search of what was said in a peer's sessions (see [[*Searching what was said there, as built][after]]), clones (see [[*Clones, as built][Clones, as built]]) and new sessions on another host (see [[*New sessions on other hosts, as built][New sessions on other hosts, as built]]): drover on one machine lists, opens and acts on the sessions of drover on the others, each host the authority over the sessions it runs.  It builds on spyglass for connections and live buffers, and on a version of opencode.el talking to several OpenCode services at once.

* What was decided

- Each session has a host; the owner host is the truth of its sessions: status, seen, archive, scheduled prompts.  Peers mirror; every change goes to the owner.
- Each host shares only the sessions it owns: nothing learnt from a peer is passed on, so two hosts connected to each other never loop nor duplicate.
- Remote OpenCode sessions open as full local buffers: opencode.el talks to the remote service through its owner's drover, which relays its requests and event stream (a tunnel at first, see [[*The fourth milestone, as built][the fourth milestone]]), and so supports several services.
- Remote terminals and shells (Claude Code in claude-code-ide on Ghostel, Eat or vterm; agent-shell) open as spyglass mirrors.
- A remote session can be cloned or forked here.
- View settings (grouping, filter, collapsed groups, subagents) stay each machine's own.
- Disconnects and reconnects cost no lag: asynchronous, a snapshot then numbered updates, resynchronized on reconnection; a first step toward controlling sessions from a phone.
- SSH from Reaver is =myssh=, never =ssh=.

* Hosts and identity

** Names

Each Emacs names its host with =drover-host=, a short lowercase name, =reaver= or =core=, by default the system's name up to its first dot, downcased; =drover-host-aliases= are other names it answers to in links.  The name travels in the peer protocol's greeting, and a peer announcing the name of this host, or a name another connected peer has, is refused: one Emacs per host serves that host's sessions.  A second Emacs on the same host, a test or a daemon, runs no peer layer unless told to with a name of its own.

=drover-host-labels= maps those identifiers to display labels for local and remote hosts alike, for example =(("reaver" . "Reaver") ("core" . "Core"))= on both machines.  =drover-host-label= resolves every label, including the remote layer's status and messages; a peer's =:label= remains a fallback, then its canonical name.  Nil and this host's aliases resolve to this host's entry, a peer's aliases to its entry.  Labels never change identifiers, session keys or protocol host names, and local labels work without the remote layer enabled.

** Peers

A peer is a host this Emacs exchanges sessions with, configured once per machine:

#+begin_src elisp
  (setq drover-host "reaver"
        drover-host-labels '(("reaver" . "Reaver") ("core" . "Core"))
        drover-remote-peers
        '(("core" :dial t :ssh "core" :program "myssh"
           :access full :aliases ("core.local" "192.168.4.22")
           :directories (("C:/Users/val/" . "~/")))))
#+end_src

=:dial= has this Emacs connect to the peer through =:program= (=myssh= on Reaver; =:ssh= the host as the SSH configuration names it); without it, it waits for the peer to connect.  =:access= is what the peer may do with this host's sessions (see [[*Security][Security]]), =:directories= maps the peer's directories to this host's for clones.  A peer connecting in under a name not configured is refused unless =drover-remote-accept-unknown= says how (nil by default).

** Sessions with a host

Every peer is a backend, =remote:core=, registered as the peer is configured: its sessions are the owner's records as last received, its operations forward to the owner, so the index, the lists, the menus and actions work as for any backend, and the index's incremental machinery (=:session= by identifier, changes coalesced) serves remote sessions unchanged.  Two slots join =drover-session=, last as new slots go: =host=, the owner's name, nil on this host, and =origin=, the owner's backend, such as =opencode= or =claude-code-ide=, which picks how a session opens here; =drover-session-backend= of a remote record is its proxy.  A remote record's =id= is the owner's key, (BACKEND . ID), so its key here is =(remote:core BACKEND . ID)=, and its parent the owner's parent key, found among that proxy's sessions.

Records of the same conversation merge by =drover-session-engine-key=, which stays =(ENGINE . ID)= on this host and becomes =(HOST ENGINE . ID)= for another: a clone keeping its identifier, an OpenCode import or a Codex rollout copied, is a session of each host, never merged with its original.  drover's own archive, =(ENGINE . ID)= in =archive.eld=, keeps only this host's conversations: a remote session is archived by its owner.  =drover-session= changing layout, the index and lists are made anew once, with =drover-stale-layout=.

** Links and scheduled prompts

=drover-links-conversation= of a remote session gives its host, the owner's canonical name, so a link stored here is =agent-session://core/claude-code/ID=; a link naming =drover-host= or one of its aliases is this host's, so a link stored on Core opens on Core as on Reaver.  Following a link with another host finds the proxy by name or alias and opens the session there, through its owner (=:open=); a peer not connected is a =user-error= naming it and when it was last heard from, and a conversation the owner does not list live is resumed by the owner, which may resume it in a front end of its own.

A scheduled prompt for a remote conversation lives in its owner's queue: scheduling one here sends it to the owner (=schedule=), the time read here and sent absolute, the triggers offered being the owner's (=triggers=).  =drover-schedule-list= shows this host's queue and each connected peer's, read from the protocol, cancelling, retrying and sending now forwarded.  A job of this host's queue naming another host, stored before this, is handed to its owner once it connects, and leaves the queue once the owner confirmed it took it.

* The peer protocol

** On spyglass connections

Drover speaks over spyglass's connections rather than beside them: a channel of messages, =drover=, on the connection that carries the mirrors of the same pair of hosts.

| What                                                                     | On spyglass (chosen)                               | A server of drover's own                |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------|
| Listener, token, registration file, handshake, frame limits              | reused                                             | written again                           |
| Transport through =myssh -W=, reconnection, adoption of an orphan stream | reused                                             | written again                           |
| Heartbeats, round trip, clock offset                                     | reused: drover corrects remote times by the offset | written again                           |
| Mirrors of the same peer                                                 | the same connection, one SSH process               | a second connection                     |
| Cost                                                                     | spyglass gains a public channel API                | duplicated, and drifting, protocol code |

Spyglass registers message handlers privately (=spyglass--register-handler=) and answers one request, =list=.  It gains, as a library change of its own: =spyglass-register-channel= (a name and a handler of a connection, a header and a payload), =spyglass-channel-send= and =spyglass-channel-request= (with a reply or an error, and nil when the stream ends first), the attach and detach hooks made public, a share visible to some peers only (=:peers=, a predicate of a connection) and input functions told which connection typed (for per-peer access), and payloads sent in chunks paced by acknowledgments (for clones).  The handshake's =app= stays =spyglass=: drover's own greeting follows on its channel.

** One channel per pair of hosts

Either host may dial; when both do, two spyglass connections join the same pair.  Each drover greeting carries the host name, the Emacs's spyglass instance and which end dialed, so both ends see both connections and keep the same one: the one dialed by the host whose name sorts first.  The other end's dialer, having lost, closes its connection with =bye= and stands by, dialing again only once the kept connection has been away for =drover-remote-standby-delay= seconds (30 by default).  Sessions go both ways over the kept connection, each host sending only its own: a host serving its index leaves out every record with a host, so nothing comes back to where it came from.

** Messages

On the =drover= channel, headers are JSON, as spyglass's are; texts with faces use spyglass's encoding of Lisp values, sanitized on arrival.

| Type        | From   | Meaning                                                                                                                                           |
|-------------+--------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =hello=     | both   | drover's protocol version, host name, instance, which end dialed, the access granted to the peer, the operations and engines this host serves     |
| =sub=       | viewer | subscribe to the owner's sessions, with the epoch and revision it holds, if any                                                                   |
| =snap=      | owner  | a chunk of a snapshot: epoch, revision, up to 200 records, =last= on the final chunk                                                              |
| =up=        | owner  | an update: the revision range =(FROM . TO)=, records changed, keys gone                                                                           |
| =req=/=res= | both   | requests with an identifier: actions, recaps, previews, older and archived sessions, engines and models, triggers, the scheduled prompts, exports |
| =jobs=      | owner  | the scheduled prompts, sent on subscription and as the queue changes                                                                              |
| =svc=       | owner  | how to reach its OpenCode service: the port, the password, the service's identifier and process, to a peer of full access only (see below)        |
| =bye=       | both   | the channel ends: the peer's configuration dropped this host                                                                                      |

** Records on the wire

An owner sends a record as an object of the slots of =drover-session=, by name, unknown names ignored on arrival so that either end may be newer, its key, and what this host's views need of it without asking: =ops=, the operations =drover-supports-p= allows for it among those forwarded, which the proxy's =:supports= reads, so that menus grey out what the owner would refuse; =set=, which of its options the owner can change, which the proxy's =:settable= reads; =buffer=, whether the owner has a buffer for it and of which kind (=opencode=, =terminal=, =shell=); =link=, its conversation.  Records are bounded where the owner reads them and again on arrival, through =drover--bound-record=; =agent-display= and the provider's display come as strings with faces, sanitized by spyglass's sanitizers, the plain =agent= and =provider-name= always beside them for clients without faces.

Times (=since=, =updated=, =created=, =cache-expires=, =archived=) are the owner's clock; the viewer subtracts the connection's clock offset, which spyglass measures from its heartbeats, so that ages read right between hosts whose clocks differ.  The order of last activity keeps the tolerance of =drover-sort-activity-tolerance= against these corrected times.

** Snapshot, revisions, resynchronization

The owner numbers changes: a revision counter it raises each time its index takes a session's record in or lets one go, kept per key (=rev=), and an epoch, made anew when its Emacs starts or its index is made anew.  It follows =drover-changed-hook=, reading the records of =drover-changed-sessions= from its index (all of them, compared by =eq= with those last sent, when the hook says t), and sends one =up= per peer per refresh, coalesced as the hook is.  The viewer applies an update only when its =FROM= is the revision it holds, so a gap, which a reliable stream never shows, has it subscribe again.  Gone keys leave tombstones, the revision they went at, kept for the last 4096 keys gone.

On reconnection the viewer subscribes with the epoch and revision it holds: under the same epoch, with tombstones reaching back that far, the owner sends the records whose =rev= is newer and the keys gone since, in one update; otherwise, a snapshot.  A resynchronization costs what changed meanwhile, a few records for a short outage, a snapshot at worst; no log of updates is kept, so nothing per peer grows while it is away.  The viewer keeps its records while the snapshot arrives, as opencode.el's registry does through a rebuild, and drops at its last chunk the keys it did not list, so that lists never empty.

** Actions, decided by the owner

The viewer never changes a remote record itself: an action is a request, its outcome the owner's records changing, its answer telling success, a refusal or an error, on one bounded line.  Each action carries an identifier the viewer makes; the owner remembers the outcome of the last 256, so an action sent again after a reconnection, its answer lost, gets that outcome rather than run twice; one whose answer never came, the connection gone for good, is reported as unknown, as a scheduled prompt interrupted is.

| Action                              | On the owner                                                                                                                      | Notes                                                                                 |
|-------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =open=                              | answers how the session opens here: its OpenCode service, or a share to mirror, sharing the buffer, or resuming the session first | see the two parts below                                                               |
| =interrupt=, =compact=, =mark-seen= | the backend's operation                                                                                                           |                                                                                       |
| =archive=, =unarchive=              | the backend's, or drover's own archive there; the owner closes its own buffer as archiving does                                   | the viewer closes its own buffer or mirror once the owner's record says archived      |
| =delete=                            | the backend's                                                                                                                     | the viewer asks the user, naming the host; the owner never asks                       |
| =rename=                            | the backend's, with the title read here                                                                                           |                                                                                       |
| =fork=                              | the backend's, its buffer kept off the owner's windows                                                                            | the viewer then opens the fork; forking here is a clone (see [[*Clone or fork here][Clone or fork here]])      |
| =send=                              | =:check= skipped, then =:apply-options= and =:send=                                                                               | from =*drover*= to a remote target; =:check= asks the user, which only the viewer can |
| =create=                            | =:create= in a directory of the owner's, with the start options                                                                   | built as =new=: see [[*New sessions on other hosts, as built][New sessions on other hosts]]                    |
| =schedule=, =unschedule=, =retry=   | the queue's own functions                                                                                                         |                                                                                       |
| =seen=                              | a mirror shown in a selected window is a session viewed, sent at most once a second                                               | the owner's notion of unseen turns follows the user wherever they look                |

Nothing a peer asks prompts on the owner, nor takes the owner's windows: what the owner does for a peer, its actions, its options, the sessions it starts or resumes, the prompts it schedules and the keys typed in a mirror, runs in =drover-quietly=, =display-buffer-overriding-action= bound to show no window, Ghostel's terminals going to the parking frame (see [[*Size and Ghostel's parking][below]]), =inhibit-interaction= making a question signal, its outcome then telling that it asked, and =use-dialog-box= nil, since a dialog box ignores =inhibit-interaction=.  Before this, only windows were kept: a key typed in a mirror ran the shell's command, which may ask, and a question asked out of a command, from a timer or a process's filter, takes a dialog box once the last event was not a key, as file notifications leave it, which on Windows stops Emacs whole, timers and processes too, the connection to its peers silent until answered.  A question a front end asks later, of its own, is no action's: agent-shell asked, from the filter reading its agent, whether to send the prompts it queued once a turn drover interrupted ended, until drover set them aside meanwhile (see [[file:design.org::*Asking nobody][Asking nobody]] in design.org).  =:respond=, which answers a request from the session's buffer, is not forwarded: the viewer opens the session, buffer or mirror, where the user answers.

** Older and archived sessions, recaps, previews

The proxy's =:history= and =:archived= ask the owner, which fetches from its backends as =drover-set-show-history= does and answers in chunks; =:recap= and =:preview= ask for one session's, bounded on both ends; =:prefetch= sends the keys a list shows, at most once a second, so that the owner fetches their context sizes and the updates bring them; =:prefetch-changes= sends them alike, as =chg=, so that the owner reads what they changed (see [[*What sessions changed][What sessions changed]]).  Every request carries the generation the viewer asked under, and an answer of a stale generation is dropped, as for local fetches.

A record carries the summary its owner's model wrote of the session's last turn, =brief= and =brief-recap= (see =drover-summary.el=): a viewer never summarizes a peer's session, whose text so leaves its host only through its owner's model.

** Lifecycle

| State          | Seen here                                                                           | Leaves for                                                                             |
|----------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =connecting=   | rows of the last snapshot, if any, stale                                            | =open= after both greetings, =reconnecting= on failure                                 |
| =open=         | live rows; updates                                                                  | =reconnecting= as the stream ends, falls silent or a write on it fails                 |
| =reconnecting= | stale rows, last heard from; =drover-remote-status= tells the attempt and the error | =open=, resynchronized; spyglass retries after 0.2, 1, 2, 5, 10, 30 s, then every 30 s |
| =standby=      | the peer's rows through the connection it dialed                                    | dials again after =drover-remote-standby-delay= without that connection                |
| =closed=       | rows dropped                                                                        | the peer removed from the configuration, or =bye=                                      |

A stream ending is seen at once; a silent one after spyglass's heartbeat timeout, 40 s, which drover's connections lower to 20 (a heartbeat every 5 s costs nothing on a local network).  Spyglass looks at a connection every half interval, and drover gives it the interval as a float: an integer halved is a whole number, and the tests' interval of 1 s halved to 0, a timer that ran once, so that a peer stopped by SIGSTOP stayed open for as long as it was stopped (=drover-remote-test-frozen-peer=).  A Mac waking from sleep finds its stream dead at the first write or heartbeat and dials again 0.2 s later.

** Never blocking

Everything goes through processes and timers: SSH runs as =make-process=, the stream's filter only splits frames, and drover's handlers do their work in slices.  A snapshot is applied 8 ms at a time from timers, as drover reads transcripts, giving way to input, the proxy reporting its sessions changed once per chunk; the owner serializes records 4 ms at a time, spyglass's tick, and stops sending to a peer whose frames are not acknowledged past spyglass's window.  Handlers run in =drover-with-reporting=; spyglass's errors reach =drover-warn= once per =drover-error-interval=; no request is waited for in a command but those a command must answer from, with a timeout, as opencode.el's =opencode-wait=, and those stop early (=drover-remote--await=): once the peer has been silent twice its heartbeat interval, a peer frozen as by a question in a dialog box answering nothing until its connection drops, past the heartbeat's timeout, or once a key is typed, read next, the echo area telling the wait past a second.

* Remote OpenCode sessions as full buffers

** Three ways

| What                   | opencode.el per service, through a tunnel (chosen)              | A mirror of the owner's buffer                       | opencode.el's requests relayed by the peer protocol |
|------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| The buffer             | a full local buffer: typing, pickers, folding, links, all local | the owner's rendering; each key travels to the owner | a full local buffer                                 |
| The owner's Emacs      | not involved: the service answers                               | must hold the buffer, render and capture it          | relays every byte, the event stream included        |
| The owner's Emacs down | works while the service runs                                    | nothing                                              | nothing                                             |
| What it needs          | opencode.el with several services, an SSH forward               | nothing more than terminals need                     | several services, and a relay of HTTP in Lisp       |
| Use                    | from milestone 5, peers of full access                          | until then, and for peers of lesser access           | rejected                                            |

The service is the authority over an OpenCode session, and the owner's drover reads it through its own registry: a turn sent, a request answered, a view posted from Reaver's buffer reach Core's service, whose events Core's registry follows, whose records Core's drover sends back.  Owner authority holds without drover relaying anything.

** Reaching the service

The host that dials a peer also runs its forwards, one SSH process per peer beside the stream (=-W= excludes other forwards): =myssh -N -o ExitOnForwardFailure=yes -L 127.0.0.1:LOCAL:127.0.0.1:PORT core=, PORT the peer's service as its =svc= message tells, LOCAL a free port of this host, bound to the loopback only.  When the other end dials, it forwards its own service to this host instead: =-R 127.0.0.1:0:127.0.0.1:PORT=, which has sshd pick the port and print it, sent in =svc=; so a single working direction of SSH serves both hosts' services, and the bytes go through sshd, not through either Emacs.  The forward starts as the first remote OpenCode buffer of that peer opens, follows the service's port when =svc= tells it changed (a service restarted), and stops ten minutes after its last buffer closed.

The password comes in =svc=, to peers of full access only, over the authenticated, encrypted stream; it is held in memory, never written.  A peer this host can reach by SSH may also read the service's registration itself, =myssh core cat ~/.local/state/opencode/service.json=, as spyglass reads its own: buffers then open while the owner's Emacs is down, the service running.

** opencode.el with several services

opencode.el gains a service object, =opencode-service=: a name, a kind (=local=, found and started through the registration file as today; =remote=, given by a provider function and never started, restarted or stopped by Emacs), its URL, password, identifier, process and version, and, as slots, the state its modules keep globally today: the lookup counter and waiters, the event stream (process, backoff, watchdog, queue), the registry (sessions, children, revision, readiness, listing, caches, contexts), the catalogs and their queue, the session buffers, the directory names.  =opencode-current-service= is the service a piece of code works for: buffer-local in a session buffer, bound around every callback, event handler and hook run for a service, the local service otherwise.  So the 15 places reading =opencode-session-buffers= by identifier, the catalogs read at redisplay and the hooks drover follows keep their signatures, and read the service they run under.

The local service is the default everywhere: without a remote service registered, opencode.el behaves and costs as today, =opencode-session-buffers= stays the local service's table, and its tests run unchanged.  A remote service is registered with =opencode-service-register= (a name, a provider of its URL and password) and dropped with =opencode-service-unregister=, which closes its stream and leaves its buffers stale, saying so in their header.

** drover's OpenCode backend and hosts

The =opencode= backend lists the local service's registry only; remote OpenCode sessions come from their owner, as any remote session.  Opening one (=:open= of the proxy, =origin= =opencode=) registers the peer's service in opencode.el, as =core=, starts the forward, and opens the session there; =drover-session-buffer= of the record is that buffer, so titles tell where it shows.  In a remote buffer, =drover-opencode--buffer-session= returns the proxy's record, so drover's keys act through the owner, while opencode.el's own keys talk to the service directly; scheduling from it goes to the owner's queue.  opencode.el's buffer hooks, run with the service bound, have the backend report the proxy's session changed rather than its own.

* Remote terminals and shells as mirrors

** Sharing on the owner

A session's buffer is shared when a peer opens it, not before: the owner's =open= shares it with spyglass under =drover/BACKEND/ID=, visible to that peer alone (=:peers=), read-only for a peer of read access, and answers the share's identifier.  A share costs nothing until subscribed, and capture starts with the first subscription; the owner unshares once no peer has been subscribed for spyglass's resume timeout, five minutes, and as the buffer dies, which closes the mirrors with a reason.  A session without a buffer on the owner is resumed there first, by its backend or a resumer (=drover-resume-with=), without taking a window.

| Origin                 | Opens here as                                                                                                                                  |
|------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =opencode=             | a full buffer through the service (milestone 5), until then and for lesser access a mirror of the owner's OpenCode buffer, opened hidden there |
| =claude-code-ide=      | a mirror of the terminal                                                                                                                       |
| =agent-shell=          | a mirror of the shell                                                                                                                          |
| =claude-code=, =codex= | resumed on the owner in its front end, then mirrored; or cloned here, when asked (see [[*Clone or fork here][Clone or fork here]])                                      |

** Input

A mirror sends keys as typed: printing characters, =RET=, =TAB=, =DEL=, arrows, =ESC=, and spyglass's =C-c= keys; a terminal's adapter types them through the terminal's own encoding, a shell's through =spyglass-submit-function=, which drover sets to =agent-shell-submit= in agent-shell buffers it shares.  Prompts written in =*drover*= go whole through =send=, not key by key, and so do scheduled prompts, delivered by the owner.  A mirror is a session buffer: drover sets =drover-session-at-point-function= there to the proxy's record, so the session keys act on the remote session from its mirror.

** Size and Ghostel's parking

A terminal mirrored takes the size of the viewer's window while a viewer shows it and no window of the owner does, as the parking branch of spyglass lets a viewer drive it; shown on both, the owner's size wins and the mirror shows it as is.  Ghostel renders only buffers a window shows: a terminal resumed or kept hidden on the owner for a viewer goes to spyglass's parking frame, invisible, where it renders at the viewer's size, and leaves it when the owner shows it again.  Drover uses whatever API that branch settles on, and the claude-code-ide backend's statuses, read from Ghostel's title, keep working in the parking frame.

** Closing

Killing a mirror closes it: the session runs on, and its owner unshares once the resume timeout passed.  Archiving or deleting a remote session closes its mirror once the owner's record says so; a mirror whose share ended says why in its header line and stays readable.

* Clone or fork here

A clone is a new conversation on this host carrying a remote conversation's history, which then goes its own way, the remote one untouched: a fork across hosts.  Built otherwise in places: see [[*Clones, as built][Clones, as built]].  The working tree is not copied: the clone runs in this host's copy of the project, =:directories= mapping the owner's directory to this host's, else asked, the owner's branch shown so that the user checks it out.  Files go through the protocol, in chunks paced by acknowledgments, so a clone works in either direction of SSH; a peer of read access may clone, as it reads the conversation anyway.

** What a peer may do

| Access | May                                                                                                |
|--------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =read= | subscribe, recaps, previews, older and archived sessions, the queue, read-only mirrors, clones     |
| =act=  | and every action but =delete=; mirrors taking input; OpenCode through a mirror, never its password |
| =full= | and =delete=, and the OpenCode service's password                                                  |

The access is the owner's, per peer, checked on every request it receives, whatever the peer's interface greyed out.  It protects what SSH does not: a peer able to log into this host by SSH can do anything there anyway, so for it =read= guards against mistakes, not against the peer; it matters for a peer this host dialed, whose own SSH access ends at the restricted key below, and for a phone.

** Setting up a direction

The first milestone checks each configured direction from the Emacs that dials, with =BatchMode=yes=, and tells what fails: SSH reaching the host, the registration file read, the stream opened.  For Reaver to Core, =myssh core= works today.  For Core to Reaver, Emacs on Core needs a key Reaver accepts, reachable without a prompt: =SSH_AUTH_SOCK= in Emacs's environment pointing to an agent holding it (Core has =~/.ssh/agent.sock=), or a key of its own; the program is MSYS's =/usr/bin/ssh= unless =myssh= exists there.  The key recommended is one for drover alone, restricted in Reaver's =authorized_keys=: =restrict,port-forwarding,permitopen="127.0.0.1:*",permitlisten="127.0.0.1:*",command="drover-peer-files"=, the command serving only the two registration files, so that Core reaching Reaver gets streams and forwards, not a shell.

** Windows

Core's Emacs runs native processes: SSH's stdin and stdout carry binary frames, which spyglass sets binary on both ends; =ssh.exe= of Windows's OpenSSH and MSYS's =ssh= differ in their agent and configuration, hence =:program= per peer; directories are =C:/…=, the drive letter upcased as OpenCode spells it, and mapped for clones.  The remote shell is MSYS bash on Core, so =cat ~/…= reads the files; PowerShell knows =cat= too.

* Open questions

1. *Host names*: =drover-host= set in each machine's configuration, or the system's name by default?  Recommended: the default, short and downcased (=reaver=, =core=), set explicitly only where it differs.
2. *Who dials*: Reaver alone, or Core too?  Recommended: Reaver dials Core now; Core also dials once its key is set up, the channel kept being Core's by the order of names, Reaver's dialing standing by.  Both are supported.
3. *SSH on Core*: there is no =myssh= on Core; is =:program= MSYS's =ssh= there, with =SSH_AUTH_SOCK= set for Emacs, or should =myssh= be installed on Core?  Recommended: MSYS's =ssh= with a dedicated, restricted key in Reaver's =authorized_keys=.
4. *Access between Reaver and Core*: recommended =full= both ways; =read= and =act= exist for later peers and the phone.
5. *Stale rows*: listed dimmed but left out of the mode-line count, as proposed, or counted?  Recommended: left out.
6. *Remote rows across restarts*: kept in memory only, as proposed, or cached in =drover-state-directory=?  Recommended: memory only; the peer resends them as it connects.
7. *Remote OpenCode registries*: following only sessions with buffers, as proposed, or every session of the remote service?  Recommended: only those with buffers; drover's list comes from the owner anyway.
8. *The owner's Emacs down*: should OpenCode buffers open from the registration file read over SSH?  Recommended: yes, for peers this host can log into.
9. *Sharing*: lazily as a peer opens, as proposed, or every session buffer at once?  Recommended: lazily.
10. *Terminal sizes*: the viewer's size while the owner does not show the terminal, the owner's otherwise?  Recommended: yes.
11. *Fork of a remote session*: one key, asking where (on its host or here), or here only under a prefix argument?  Recommended: asking, here first, since forking on the host stays possible.
12. *Clone of Claude Code*: staged then forked by the CLI, as proposed, or copied under a new identifier by rewriting =sessionId=?  Recommended: forked by the CLI, which drover already does, once checked by hand.
13. *Directories for clones*: =:directories= per peer, asked when unmapped?  Recommended: yes, the answer offered to be saved.
14. *The host column*: only when a remote session is listed, as proposed, or always?  Recommended: only then.
15. *New sessions on another host* (milestone 7): wanted before OpenCode buffers, after, or not?  Recommended: after milestone 5.
16. *Where the code lives*: =drover-remote.el= in drover, spyglass optional, as proposed, or a package of its own?  Recommended: in drover.
17. *The gateway*: which host runs it, and what the phone talks to, a web page or an app?  Recommended: decided at milestone 8; nothing before depends on it.

** The protocol, compatible

The version stays 1: a peer of an older drover refuses another version, and keeps working with this one.  What is new is told in the greeting's =caps=, =actions=, =bye= and =grant=, and sent only to peers telling them, or harmless to the others.

| What               | New                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
|--------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =hello=            | =caps=, =epoch=, the owner's epoch, for the history                                                                                                                                                                                |
| =grant=            | after a greeting, the access this host grants the peer: the peer greys out what it forbids                                                                                                                                         |
| records            | =ops=, what the owner performs on the session for a peer, whatever its access: =interrupt=, =mark-seen=, =archive=, =unarchive=, =delete=, =rename=, =answer=, =send=; kept beside the record, never in it                         |
| request =act=      | an identifier, an action, up to 100 keys, its arguments; answered with =out=, one outcome per key, =t= or a line                                                                                                                   |
| request =requests= | what the pending requests of a session ask, as data, for the viewer to read the answer                                                                                                                                             |
| =bye=              | =kind=: =restart= and =quit= leave the peer's rows, dimmed for a quit at once, kept through a restart's grace; =off= and =dropped= close it for good, as every farewell did; an older peer is told only of those, and of a restart |

A peer of an older drover lists its sessions here as before: its sessions' menus grey out what it cannot do, and acting on one says that peer runs an older drover, to be updated (=:unsupported=, a backend property telling why an operation does not apply).

** Actions

The viewer never changes a remote record: each action is a request, the owner's backends perform it as for its own user, without a window nor a prompt, and the rows change when its records do.

| Action                 | On the owner                                                                                                                                                                                         | Here                                                                                                                                            |
|------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =mark-seen=            | the backend's =:mark-seen=                                                                                                                                                                           | =s=, and opening the session (=RET=) when its turn ended unseen: Core's =done= sessions leave Reaver's count once seen here                     |
| =archive=, =unarchive= | as =drover-archive= and =drover-unarchive= do there, its own buffer closed as archiving does; a session archived already, or not archived, is done                                                   | the archived sessions fetched through the owner, as before                                                                                      |
| =interrupt=            | the backend's =:interrupt=                                                                                                                                                                           |                                                                                                                                                 |
| =delete=               | as =drover-delete= does there, without asking                                                                                                                                                        | asked here, as for a local session; =full= access only                                                                                          |
| =rename=               | the backend's =:rename=                                                                                                                                                                              |                                                                                                                                                 |
| =answer=               | the backend's =:answer=, a new operation: OpenCode's answers a permission (=once=, =always=, =reject=) or a question's fields, through opencode.el, in the session's buffer opened hidden if need be | =A=: the backend's =:requests= tells what the oldest request asks, waited for 10 s at most in the command, then the minibuffer reads the answer |
| =send=                 | the backend's =:submit=, as a scheduled prompt is delivered, a session drover archived restored first                                                                                                | the prompt's picker offers peers' sessions taking prompts, by titles and hosts, among this host's when shown here, else after them; text only   |

Scheduled prompts stay the business of the host running the session: its queue delivers through its own front ends, and a queue here could not resume nor watch a session there.  Scheduling for another host's session says so, rather than asking the owner to keep a queue of this host's (the =schedule= requests of the design wait for a later milestone).  Opening a session of a peer still shows its view, read only, now rendered: its status, harness and model in the list's faces and glyphs, its last reply and prompt Markdown fontified as opencode.el's buffers fontify it (markdown-ts, markup hidden, code blocks by their languages, through =opencode-fontify-markdown=), bounded as recaps are; =g= shows it anew.  Mirrors and full OpenCode buffers remain milestones 3 and 5.

The access is checked on the owner for every request, whatever the viewer greyed out: =read= performs nothing, =act= everything but =delete=, =full= everything; a refusal is answered on one line, noted in the history and warned once per =drover-error-interval=.  Every field is checked: the identifier's shape, the action among eight, at most 100 keys of short symbols and identifiers, a title on one line, a prompt of 256 kB at most, answers of 20 fields of short strings; the owner finds a key's record among its listed, fetched or archived sessions, or makes one of a registered backend of its own to restore or delete by identifier.

Several sessions go through the bulk machinery (=drover--bulk-start=): the proxy is a backend that batches (=:batches=), its operations of a new kind, =batched=, of which 512 await answers at once rather than =drover-bulk-requests=; each slice's operations gather into one request per action and arguments, 100 keys at most, sent once the slice ends.  300 sessions archived made 3 requests in the tests, reported once.  The owner performs a request's keys in slices of 4 ms, =drover-bulk-requests= of its backends' operations awaiting answers at once, and answers once all settled, or failed after =drover-remote-action-timeout= seconds.  With all of them in flight, the owner's runner waits for an answer to bring it back, never for a timer due at once: an interactive Emacs runs timers and redisplays again as long as a timer ran (=wait_reading_process_output=), so a runner coming back at once reads no process until the queue empties, the answers it waits for among them.  It did, and 32 of 33 OpenCode sessions marked seen from another host failed, their connections to the owner's service never seen open within opencode.el's 5 s; batch Emacs reads processes anyway, so =drover-remote-test-act-idle-while-in-flight= counts the runner's runs.

Each request has an identifier, its host's name, epoch and a counter; the owner keeps the outcomes of the last 256, and the replies awaiting one still performed: a request whose stream ended is asked again once connected anew (=drover-remote--request=), and gets the outcome rather than act twice.  One whose peer does not come back within =drover-remote-action-timeout=, 60 s, fails, saying so on one line.

The resumption seen in the live run is not a bug of resumption: a subscription holds the owner's epoch, and Core's new subscription to Reaver held Reaver's, which had not changed; resuming was right.  The bug was the other way: a remote layer started anew on a connection that stayed, as reloading a drover whose structures changed does, kept no subscription of its peer's, and the peer, greeted again on its kept connection, did not subscribe again, its rows of the restarted host frozen until the next drop.  =drover-remote-test-relayout-resubscribes= fails without the fix. ** Backing off

A peer this host dials and cannot reach is tried after =drover-remote-backoff= seconds, 0.2, 1, 2, 5, 10, 30, 60, 120, 240, then every 300, rather than spyglass's every 30: Core switched off for the night had Reaver try 945 times, each through =myssh= a process started, which on a Mac with an endpoint security agent keeps Emacs 0.1 to 0.9 s. - this host's network changes: its interfaces and addresses, =network-interface-list=, 13 µs, compared every =drover-remote-lookout-interval= (15 s);
- this host wakes from sleep: a look coming far later than the interval, timers not running while the machine sleeps;
- the user calls =drover-remote-check=.

* The fourth milestone, as built

A peer's OpenCode session opens here as a full opencode.el buffer: =RET= on its row, or on a link to it, opens =*OpenCode@core: Title*=, its transcript rendered, its input typed into, its pickers, folding, answers and pending prompts as any session buffer's, following its session as the peer's service streams it. ** How the buffer reaches the service

The plan had the host dialing a peer forward a port to the peer's service over SSH (=myssh -N -L=), the peer sending its password in =svc=.  Built, the owner's drover relays its service instead, over the spyglass connection the pair already keeps: opencode.el's requests to the service registered under the peer's name go to the relay =relay://core=, which drover carries on its channel, and the owner performs them against its own service, with its own password, and answers; the event stream goes the same way, its bytes relayed as the owner's service writes them, in pieces of whole characters of 64 kB at most.

The viewer names the sessions it offers rather than the owner applying the filter: the search here decides what it offers (the filter, the subagents shown, the older and archived sessions fetched from the owner), and the owner's =:wanted= is then exactly that set, as a local backend's is, so that the owner's ripgrep is given only those files, and a match is never spent on a session the viewer would count out of scope.  The keys are a hundred bytes a session, key and engine's identifier: 553 conversations of Core make about 60 kB, against the records of those sessions the viewer received anyway.  What the owner's backends read of the scope travels too: whether archived sessions show (OpenCode's =:archived=, Codex's archived rollouts) and the project of the filter (OpenCode's =:project=).

** The owner

A request is checked as every other (a peer greeted, of any access: searching reads, as recaps do), then each backend of its own with =:search= is asked, at once, with a =:wanted= of the sessions named; their matches go as they come, in one =srm= per answer of a backend, plain, bounded (=drover-remote--wire-match=); it ends once every backend ended, its limit reached (its backends then stopped), after =drover-remote-search-timeout= (60 s), or as the viewer says =src=.  Two searches at most run for a connection, one for the minibuffer and one for =drover-search-text=: a third stops the oldest, its viewer told.  A connection ending stops its searches; the remote layer stopping stops them all.  A backend failing is noted and told in =sre=, the others going on; the viewer then reports it as the search reports a backend failing, once, after the matches that came.

** The viewer

The proxy's =:search= (=drover-remote--search=) asks only a connected peer telling =search=, and only when the search offers some of its sessions: a peer of an older drover, or one not connected, searches nothing and is told of nowhere, as an OpenCode service without its search route.  The scope of the search gains =:sessions=, a function of a backend returning the sessions of it the search offers, in the list's order, which the proxy sends; the cancelling function the search calls as typing goes on (its debounce, =drover-search-delay=) sends =src=.  Matches are decoded as records are (=drover-remote--decode-match=): a key of short symbols and an identifier, else an engine and an identifier with the host, so that =drover-search--resolve= finds the session among the peer's here, a snippet bounded, a position an integer or a short string; anything else is dropped.  A search whose link ends fails, saying so; one whose owner does not end it past the timeout is stopped there and fails.

* Clones, as built

| What                  | New                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

* New sessions on other hosts, as built

The seventh milestone: the prompt starts a session on another host as it starts one here.  Its target picker offers, after this host's new sessions, a new session of each harness each connected peer starts for this host, =[New OpenCode session on Core…]=, =[New Claude Code IDE session on Core…]=, =[New agent-shell session (Codex) on Core…]=; choosing one asks for the directory there, the prompt's directory mapped (below), and the peer starts the session through its own backend, in no window, hands it the prompt and answers its key; the prompt then leaves =*drover*= and the session opens here once the peer's records list it: an OpenCode session in its full =*OpenCode@core: Title*= buffer through the relay, a terminal or a shell mirrored, live.  It does not start, the peer refusing, failing or going away: one line says why, and the prompt stays in =*drover*=, with the host and directory chosen, for another try.

** Directories there

The directory there defaults to this host's =:directories= of that peer, =(THEIRS . OURS)=, read backwards (a directory under OURS goes under THEIRS), else what the peer maps it to with its own =:directories= for this host, as for clones, else the same directory under its home as under this host's, else its home.  It is read with completion on the peer's directories, which the peer lists (=ls=): the names of the subdirectories of the directory typed, sorted, of its first 2000 entries, nothing else, never a file's name nor what any file holds, each listing kept 30 s; the last component completes as file names do (the table's boundaries), so that every completion style and UI works; without a slash, the directories the peer's sessions run in, the latest active first.  No TRAMP, no SSH command: the peer's drover answers.  A directory there that does not exist is refused there.

** Catalogs

What a peer starts, its engines' agents, models and defaults, are the peer's, asked of it (=catalogs=) and kept =drover-remote-catalog-ttl= (300 s), asked again past it, the old answer used meanwhile: what it starts as it greets (=grant=), the counterpart of a directory of this host when asked for one, the catalogs of its engines in a directory of its when asked for one.  The owner asks its own engines as its prompt does: each engine's =:defaults= for the directory, the catalogs of a service fetched without waiting in a request handler, =drover-remote-catalog-timeout= (10 s) at most; then each engine's agents (=:agents=), models (its first model source, as =drover-models= reads them) and what a session started without a choice runs (=:resolve=); an engine whose defaults did not come tells only the models it remembers, since reading more would wait.  The harnesses it starts are its prompt's new sessions (=drover-prompt-new-targets=) whose front end is installed, then those of its other backends with =:create=.  Models travel as their provider, identifier, names, variants with their efforts, default variant and engines, and are found again on the owner by provider and identifier, among those it told or remembers, with the names its front ends take for them (aliases), which do not travel.

| capability =usage= | told by a host totalling its usage for its peers                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| request =usage=    | a period, =today=, =week=, =month= or =all=, and the dimensions wanted, among =session=, =day=, =harness=, =provider=, =model= and =project=: answered with rows of those, the others summed, 4000 at most and how many were left out, and whether it still reads usage it had not read; =read= access |

A row is a vector: the engine, the session's identifier and its parent's, the day, the provider, the model, the project, the four counts of tokens, the cost or false, of an =included= row what it would have cost at list prices, which an older drover ignores, the billing, =priced= or =included=, where the cost came from, and the session's title.  Each is checked as it comes (=drover-usage-decode-row=): an engine of a short name, strings bounded to 200 characters, counts natural numbers, a cost finite, a billing one of the two; a row that does not fit is left out.  The report asks each peer connected for the period it shows, at most once a minute, every 5 s while the peer still reads, and a session of a peer asks it for every session's totals ever; what was received is kept in memory, by host and request, as last received, a host not connected showing as of then.  An older drover, without the capability, is not asked.

The test of the remote layer starts the agent with a Claude Code home of a conversation of three replies, two records of one, and checks the period's totals and the session's, its tokens and its cost as the agent priced it, through 127.0.0.1.

* What sessions changed

Each host reads what its own sessions changed in all their turns (=drover-changes=, see =doc/design.org=), from its own engines, for the sessions its lists show and those its peers' lists show: a peer never reads another host's transcripts, nor asks another host's service.

| Protocol             | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
|----------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| capability =changes= | told by a host reading what its sessions changed for its peers                                                                                                                                          |
| message =chg=        | the keys of the sessions a peer's lists show, 500 at most, sent with those to prefetch (=pre=), at most once a second, again once connected anew; only to a host telling the capability, from any access |
| record =:changes=    | =[ADDITIONS DELETIONS FILES]=, the lines added and removed, false when the engine counted none, and the files; left out while unknown or nothing                                                        |

The owner takes =chg= as its own lists' asking (=drover-prefetch= of the kind =changes=), for the sessions it serves alone: it reads them once settled, and again as their turns end; the counts change its records, which its updates bring, and its recaps tell the files, as =drover-recap= tells them there.  What a peer sends is checked as it comes (=drover-remote--decode-totals=, =drover-remote--decode-files=): counts natural numbers, files a positive one, names lines. The test of the remote layer has the agent's fake engine tell what its sessions changed, a peer ask for three of them, and checks the counts its records bring, the files its recap tells, and a session at work read once its turn ends.

* Tasks of other hosts, as built

A task belongs to the host running its sessions (see design.org, [[file:design.org::*Tasks][Tasks]]): its worktree is in that host's checkout of the repository, its setup runs there, its sessions are that host's.  Its heading is wherever the user keeps it, on the host asking, which writes =DROVER_HOST= with the owner's name, so that finishing goes to the owner.  Peers telling the capability =task= take one request, =task=, of two kinds:

| Request          | What it carries                                                                                                       | What the owner does                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =task= =start=   | the task's ID, title, Jira key and body, a repository's name, =worktree= or =in-place=, a name, a base, whether to carry changes | for a peer of =act= or =full= access, with a repository of that name in its =drover-task-repositories= and a checkout there: makes the place as it would for itself, in no window, starts a session there through its harness, in none either, and answers where (worktree, branch, base) and the session's key; a setup failing answers where, and why |
| =task= =finish=  | the task's ID, its worktree and branch there, its repository's name, its pull request if any, whether to discard what is uncommitted | for a peer of =full= access, only a worktree of that repository whose Git directory names that task: tells what is uncommitted unless told to discard it, then finishes as its own settings say, never prompting, and answers what was done                     |

Every value is checked as received: a name of a known shape, a base of one, a worktree that is a linked worktree of the checkout named, whose marker names the task; never a path the owner does not hold for that task.  A request is asked once, not again after a stream dropped, so that a task never starts twice; the asking host tells why it did not answer.  A peer of an older drover is not asked, and the user is told why.  The test of the remote layer starts a task on the agent in a repository made for it, commits there, finishes it, and checks that a dirty worktree is told and kept, that another task's worktree is refused, and that a peer of =act= access finishes nothing.

* The phone, as built

The eighth milestone, otherwise than planned: the Android client (=drover-android=, a repository of its own) is no gateway's client but a peer of each host, as hosts are of each other.  It dials each host through SSH, reads spyglass's registration with an exec and opens a =direct-tcpip= channel to its listener, as =ssh -W= does, and speaks spyglass's frames and drover's channel; its key lives in the phone's hardware.  Each host stays the authority over its sessions: the phone's actions are requests, checked against its =:access=.

| Design (Mobile later)                                    | Built                                                                                   | Why                                                                                                                                  |
|----------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| a gateway, a viewer of every host, serving the phone     | the phone a peer of each host, owning no sessions                                       | the protocol is spoken once more, in Kotlin, rather than relayed by a service of its own; access stays per host, its owner's        |
| HTTPS or a WebSocket behind the homelab's reverse proxy | SSH to each host through the homelab's WireGuard, a key restricted to the listener      | nothing more listens; SSH authenticates both ends                                                                                    |
| the gateway pushing what needs the user                  | each host pushing its own, encrypted, through UnifiedPush (an ntfy server)              | a host knows its sessions first; nothing is relayed                                                                                  |

** Viewers

A peer owning no sessions is a viewer: its configuration says so (=:viewer=), or its drover tells the capability =viewer=, as the phone's does.  A host serves it its sessions as to any peer, but never subscribes to it nor resubscribes as it greets again, registers no proxy backend for one configured so, lists it in no group, column nor filter, starts no session there and clones nothing to it, and refuses its clones; its stream ending dims nothing.  A peer's connections take its =:heartbeat= (the phone's 15 and 60 s, against drover's 5 and 20 on a local network), and a connection it made waits =:resume= seconds for it to come back once its stream ended (spyglass's =spyglass-connection-set-resume=), so that its mirrors resume rather than start over, a phone closing its streams as its application leaves the screen.

** Pushes

While the phone is not connected, each host pushes it what needs the user (=drover-remote-push.el=).  The phone registers on each connection: an endpoint of its UnifiedPush distributor, a topic of the homelab's ntfy, and a key of 32 random bytes per host, over the channel SSH protects.  The host posts, with its own token of the push server, a body =dp1.KID.= followed by the base64url of a nonce, the AES-256-GCM ciphertext of the payload's JSON and its tag: the push server and its cache see ciphertext only.

| What                 | Shape                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Access |
|----------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------|
| capability =push=    | told when =drover-remote-push-endpoints= is set, gnutls has AES-256-GCM and curl is found                                                                                                                                                             | -      |
| request =push=       | =set= {=url=, =key= (base64url of 32 bytes), =kid= (4 to 32 of =[A-Za-z0-9_-]=), =detail= (=full=, =titles= or =counts=), =hold= (seconds, or false)}: answered {=ok=, =kid=, =seq=, =max= 4096}; =drop= true; =test= true, answered {=st=, the push server's status} | read, a peer configured here |
| request =watch=      | =keys= (16 at most), =on=, =ttl= (seconds, 6 h at most): answered {=ok=, =watching=, the keys watched}                                                                                                                                              | read   |
| message =fg=         | =on=: the phone is in front, connected; pushed nothing meanwhile                                                                                                                                                                                       | -      |

The payload is {=v= 1, =h= the host, =s= the counter, =t= its time, =r= =need=, =clear=, =watch= or =test=, =n= how many sessions need the user, =m= true when some were left out, =e= the entries, =w= the watched sessions not among them}.  An entry is {=k= the key, =ti= the title (80 characters), =st= the status, =u= since when (the host's clock), =p= the parent's key, =g= the engine, =q= the oldest request, {=k= =permission= or =question=, =s= its summary (160), =id= its identifier and =ch= its choices when the backend tells them}, =x= the tail of the last reply or the error (200), for one done or failed}; =titles= drops =s= and =x=, =counts= the entries.  Entries go waiting, failed, done, then newest first; a body stays within 4096 bytes, ntfy's message, excerpts dropped first, then summaries, then entries.

The nonce is the first four bytes of SHA-256 of the host's name and the key's identifier, then an eight-byte counter, the additional data =drover-push/1 HOST KID=.  The counter starts from the clock in milliseconds for a new registration and is reserved 64 ahead in =push.eld=, so that a key sent again after the host lost its file never meets a nonce again.  Gnutls is asked =gnutls-available-p= before any of its cryptography, which on Windows otherwise crashes Emacs, and given a copy of the key, which it wipes.

What is pushed is this host's own sessions, not stale, whose status needs the user, at the top of their trees or waiting themselves, followed from =drover-changed-hook=: a session coming to need the user is pushed (=need=), one leaving it, seen or answered here, too (=clear=), so that the phone's notification goes; watched sessions, a turn the phone started, as their status changes (=watch=), until the turn ends.  Changes gather 2 s, 10 s at most, pushes 5 s apart to one phone.  While this Emacs saw input within the hold (=drover-remote-push-hold=, 120 s; the phone's, then the peer's =:push-hold= first), what is new waits for an idle timer and goes if it still needs the user; what left goes at once.  While the phone is in front nothing is pushed; as it leaves, what it was shown is what it saw live, its notifications reconciled from it; its stream dropping while in front, what differs from what it was last told goes.  A push is posted by =curl -K -=, the configuration, token and body on its standard input, killed past 30 s: =url-retrieve= connects synchronously on Windows.  A failure waits 10, 30, 60, then 300 s; a 404 or 410 marks the endpoint gone until the phone registers again.  =M-x drover-remote-push-test= pushes a test to each phone; =drover-remote-status= tells how pushes stand.

Tests: =drover-remote-push-test.el= seals and opens with GCM's test vector, the key not wiped and the nonce's counter rising across restarts; takes only allowlisted endpoints; pushes need, clear and watch, holds while typing, nothing in front, bounds 10001 sessions and titles of 500 kB within 4096 bytes, backs off, keeps =push.eld= and leaves one that does not read untouched, and posts through curl to a server of its own.  =drover-remote-test-push= registers this host with the agent as a phone, the agent recording what it would post: a session done, then one waiting with its request's identifier and choices, nothing while in front, a change held while typing then pushed once idle, the registration surviving a restart of the agent's remote layer, its counters rising.

** Conversations

What a session said, for the phone to draw at its width rather than a terminal's: entries read from the file its engine records, Claude Code's transcript or Codex's rollout (=drover-conversation.el=), an engine telling how through =:transcript= and =:entries= of its own or =drover-conversation-readers=.  A session of agent-shell reads through its engine; one of OpenCode reads through the relay of its service instead, and one of no engine drover reads is answered =none=, the phone mirroring its terminal.

| What                | Shape                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Access |
|---------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------|
| capability =conv=   | told by a host reading conversations for its peers                                                                                                                                                                             | -      |
| request =cv=        | =key=, =before= (the start of the first line of a page told, or none for the newest), =n= (60, 200 at most): answered {=g= the file's generation, =sz= its size, =from= the first line's offset, =more=, =to= where the lines read end, =eng=, =e= entries, oldest first}; an error =none: …= when nothing is read | read   |
| request =cvs=       | =id=, =key=, =from= (a page's =to=), =g=: answered {=ok=, =sz=}; then entries appended in =cvd= messages; 4 a connection, a fifth stopping the oldest, 16 in all                                                         | read   |
| message =cvd=       | the host: {=id=, =e=, =to=}, 256 kB of entries at most, the last of a read telling where the next line begins                                                                                                             | -      |
| message =cve=       | the host: {=id=, =why=, =reset=}: the file replaced, shrunk or gone (=reset= true, read a page anew), or the stream stopped                                                                                               | -      |
| message =cvc=       | the phone: {=id=}, stop it                                                                                                                                                                                                    | -      |

An entry is {=i= its line's byte offset, then =.N= for the Nth after the first of a line, =y= its kind, =t= its time} and, by kind: =user= and =text= =x= (32000 characters), =cut= the full length when cut; =think= =x= (4000); =tool= =c= the call's identifier, =n= the tool, =s= a line saying what it does (a command, a file relative to the session's directory, a pattern), =in= its input as JSON (4000); =res= =c=, =x= its output (8000, head and tail kept), =er=; =patch= =f= the file, =st= =added=, =deleted=, =modified= or =renamed= (=fr= from), =a= and =d= lines added and deleted, =h= its hunks (16000 characters or 400 lines); =err= =x=; =meta= =x=: =interrupted=, =compacted=, a slash command, or a record too long to show and what kind it was.  Claude Code's subagents' records (=isSidechain=) and bookkeeping are left out; Codex's messages, recorded twice, are read once, from its events.

A page reads backward a window of 64 kB at a time, in slices of 8 ms from timers, 8 MB at most; the last line, unless whole, is still being written and left for later.  A line longer than 256 kB is never parsed: its first 8 kB tell its kind and the start of its text.  A replied page stays within 512 kB, its oldest entries dropped.  Followed files are looked at by one timer every second, read forward from the offset followed, whole lines only; a file replaced (its inode) or shrunk ends the stream with =reset=.  The connection's streams stop as it ends, and all as the remote layer stops.

Tests: =drover-conversation-test.el= reads every kind of entry of both engines, pages concatenated equal to one whole read through windows of 37 bytes cutting characters of four, lines of 500 kB unparsed and of 200 kB bounded, a line being written left, appended lines followed whole and a shrunk file ending it.  =drover-remote-test-conversation= reads a conversation of the agent's Claude Code, follows a prompt appended there, stops, and a session of no engine answers =none=.

** Scheduled prompts

Prompts scheduled for a session are its host's business, as the second milestone decided: its queue delivers them through its own front ends. ** A prompt for a session whose turn runs

The owner performs =send=.  Its session running its agent in its buffer (=:runs-in-buffer=, Claude Code's and agent-shell's terminals) while its turn runs, or its buffer unable to take a prompt without touching what its input holds (its backend's =:blocker=), the prompt goes to the owner's own queue of prompts, saved: =drover-schedule-add-after-turn= while the turn runs, else =drover-schedule-deliver=, which waits until the blocker clears. The owner tells the peer, a message =note= the viewer shows on one line (=… runs a turn on Core: the prompt goes once it ends, queued there=) and keeps in the peer's history; a peer older than this ignores it.
