# AGENTS.md - drover

Emacs package gathering the sessions of coding agent front ends (opencode.el first) into one place: attention tracking, one list of sessions, and actions on any session.

## Rules

- Never evaluate code in the user's running Emacs to test this package (no `emacsclient`, no Emacs MCP evaluation). Every test runs in a separate `emacs -Q --batch` process (`make test`).
- Never start, stop or restart the user's OpenCode service, nor start or change a Claude Code or Codex session: tests write their transcripts and rollouts in throwaway homes (`test/drover-transcript-fixtures.el`), and run scripts of their own for the CLIs. Tests use the fake backend in `test/drover-test-helpers.el`, or stub the opencode.el functions they call.
- `make compile` must stay free of warnings, and `make lint` (checkdoc) clean.
- Backends are authoritative: drover renders what backends report and keeps only interface state (point, expanded sections, the last session visited, what each list drew and which sessions changed while it was hidden), the records of live sessions as backends last reported them (the index, current through their reports and listed anew now and then), the older and archived sessions a backend fetched, while they are shown, and the recaps of expanded sessions' last turns, until those sessions change.
- The list scales to thousands of sessions: a change draws only what it changes, in place (`drover-list--follow`), and must stay the list a drawing anew draws, which the tests check; `make bench` times it. In `drover-state-directory` it keeps only the models live sessions offered, the conversations it archived for backends without an archive of their own, and, as a cache checked against the files, what it read of the transcripts and rollouts engines record (`drover-transcript`).
- Byte code must not depend on optional front ends (agent-shell, claude-code-ide): their backends declare what they use and load the front end as they activate; `make compile` checks that the byte code compiled with the required packages alone is the same.
- The list's buffer shows sessions and the counts of their groups, not settings nor keys, nor a summary line: the View menu shows those, the mode line hints at them and counts what needs the user.
- Drover has one view of sessions, the list of `drover-list.el`, in two buffers that may show at once, the list of an ordinary window and the sidebar of a side window, each with its own point, sections and layout, the view settings shared; improve it rather than adding another. A change costs each list only what its own lines need: what does not depend on the layout (trees, rows, widths, recaps) is made once for both, and a hidden list draws nothing until shown. It uses the faces and glyphs of `drover-ui.el` (strings in faces through `drover-face`, so font lock keeps them) and the commands of `drover-keys.el`; it sets `drover-view-p` and `drover-session-at-point-function`, draws `drover-view-groups` (grouped by `drover-group-by`, sorted by `drover-sort-by`), and inherits `drover-session-map` in its keymap. What a line shows is decided from the window's width and `drover-list-fields`, not by special cases.
- One key, one action: a key of `drover-session-map` means the same in every list, and no key belongs to two of the sets of `drover-dispatch` (session, prompt, global). Its List group (moving in drover's list, here or beside) uses keys of no set, but RET, which opens there and sends in prompt buffers: the menu shows one RET at a time. The list binds only standard keys of its own (RET, C-o, q, copying, as `kill-ring-save` remapped, and Magit's n, p, TAB). View settings belong in the View menu, `drover-view`, not in keys of their own. The places other packages extend `drover-dispatch` from (after b and ', after p, after O) stay stable.
- Comments are sparse and explain why, not what. Docstrings are complete sentences; the first line stands alone.
